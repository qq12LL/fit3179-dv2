A quick guide for Malaysian readers

Council area

One of about 540 local councils, like a Malaysian majlis daerah. Most maps here colour each council area.

Area disadvantage

A score from the Australian Bureau of Statistics. It combines income, education, jobs and housing. Lower means more disadvantaged.

Developmentally vulnerable

Teachers assess every child in their first school year, around age five. Children in the lowest 10% of a skill area count as vulnerable.

School advantage score

Officially called ICSEA. It reflects the education and jobs of students' parents. The national average is 1,000.

Year 12

The final year of secondary school. It is similar to STPM or A-Levels in Malaysia.

Low socio-economic area

A neighbourhood in the poorest quarter of Australia, based on the area disadvantage score.

Part 1: Before school

The gap opens before the first school day

In 2024, 23.5% of children started school developmentally vulnerable.
It is the highest rate since 2009.

A record high

23.5% of five-year-olds were vulnerable in at least one skill area. In 2021 it was 22.0%.

Poverty matters

In the poorest fifth of neighbourhoods, 34.7% are vulnerable. In the richest fifth, 16.2%.

Distance matters

Very remote children: 46.1% vulnerable. Children in major cities: 22.5%.

Reading the map

Inside the big cities

Outer suburbs also score high on both. Examples are Fairfield, Hume, Logan and Playford.

Part 2: At school

Then they go to very different schools

Australia has 9,755 schools. Each has an advantage score based
on its students' families.

1,049

Median score of a major-city school. Above the national average.

712

Median score of a very remote school. The lowest of any area.

1 in 9

Schools scoring under 900, serving mostly disadvantaged families.

How to read it

Each circle is a school. Bigger circles have more students.

Purple: disadvantaged families. Navy: advantaged families.

Try it

Drag across the right-hand chart to pick a score range.

Matching schools stay coloured on the map.

What stands out

Of 169 schools scoring under 700, 87% are remote or very remote.

Three school sectors

Government schools are free. Catholic and Independent schools charge fees.

Different student mixes

Government schools spread the widest. They make up 87% of schools scoring under 900.

Independent schools sit mostly above average.

Government schools

31% of their students come from the least advantaged quarter of families.

Independent schools

Only 11% do. Their largest group is the most advantaged quarter.

Four in five

Government schools teach 80% of Australia's least advantaged students.

Part 3: Finishing school

Fewer young people finish school

Year 12 opens the door to university and skilled jobs.
Completion depends on where you live.

Most advantaged areas

About 92% of young adults finished Year 12.

Most disadvantaged areas

About 44% finished. That is less than half.

Inside the cities

Inner and northern Sydney and inner Melbourne are darkest. The outer fringes are lighter.

A clear trend

As areas become less disadvantaged, Year 12 completion rises steadily.

The far left

In the 21 most disadvantaged areas, completion ranges from 22% to 45%.

Most are remote First Nations communities.

Part 4: University

And fewer make it to university

A fair share would be 25 in 100 students
from poorer areas.

Low socio-economic areas

15.4 in 100 students, against a fair share of 25.

About 10 students in every 100 are missing.

First Nations students

2.3 in 100 students, against 3.2% of the population.

Little change since 2011

The low socio-economic share has stayed between 14% and 16%.

Regional students

Their share fell from 19.7% to 17.2%.

Remote students

Below 1% in every year since 2011.

Part 5: What it means

One story, told four times

Before school

Children in the poorest and most remote areas are two to four times as likely to be behind.

At school

Disadvantaged and remote children are concentrated in a small group of mostly government schools.

Finishing school

Year 12 completion closely follows how disadvantaged an area is.

University

Low socio-economic students remain 10 in 100 short of a fair share.

Each stage builds on the one before. Help that reaches
children early tackles the gap where it starts.
