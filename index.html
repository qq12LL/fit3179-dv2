Education equity in Australia · 2021–2025 data

The Postcode Lottery

In Australia, the place a child grows up shapes their education at every step, from their first day of school to their chance of getting into university. This page follows those steps one at a time.

A quick guide for Malaysian readers

Council area (LGA)
Australia has about 540 local councils, a similar idea to a Malaysian majlis daerah. Most of the maps here colour each council area.
Disadvantage (SEIFA)
An ABS score for each area, built from Census information on income, education, jobs and housing. A lower score means a more disadvantaged area. "Decile 1" is the most disadvantaged 10% of areas.
Developmentally vulnerable
Teachers assess every child in their first year of school (about age 5) in five areas: physical health, social skills, emotional maturity, language and thinking, and communication. A child in the bottom 10% in any area is counted as "vulnerable".
ICSEA
A score for each school that reflects the education and jobs of its students' parents. The Australian average is 1,000; schools below 900 serve mostly disadvantaged families.
Year 12
The final year of secondary school, roughly equivalent to STPM or A-Levels in Malaysia.
Low SES
"Low socio-economic status": students whose home address is in the poorest quarter (25%) of neighbourhoods.

01

The gap opens before the first day of school

By the time Australian children turn five, the place they live already predicts how ready they are for school. In 2024, 23.5% of children starting school nationally were developmentally vulnerable in at least one area. That is the highest rate since the census began in 2009.

02

Then they go to very different schools

Australia has 9,755 schools and about 4.2 million students. A school's ICSEA shows what kind of families it serves, and that changes sharply from one part of the country to another.

03

Fewer young people finish school

Finishing Year 12 opens the door to university and to most skilled jobs. Among young adults aged 25–34, about 92% finished Year 12 in the most advantaged tenth of council areas, against about 44% in the most disadvantaged tenth.

04

And fewer make it to university

If every group went to university in proportion to its size, a quarter of students would come from low-SES areas, because low-SES areas are defined as the poorest 25% of the country. In reality the share is far lower, and it has barely changed in more than a decade.

05

One story, told four times

  1. Before school: five-year-olds in the poorest and most remote areas are already two to four times as likely to be behind.
  2. At school: disadvantaged and remote children are concentrated in a small set of schools, almost all of them government schools.
  3. Finishing school: Year 12 completion closely follows how disadvantaged an area is.
  4. University: low-SES students remain about 10 percentage points short of a fair share, with almost no progress since 2011.

The gaps add up because each stage builds on the one before. Programs that reach children early, such as preschool, health checks and language support in remote and outer-suburban communities, tackle the problem where it starts.
